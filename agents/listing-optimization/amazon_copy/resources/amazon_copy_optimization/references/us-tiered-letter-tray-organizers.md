# US Tiered Letter Tray and Desk Organizer Short Fields

Use this reference for US metal tiered letter trays, paper organizers, desktop file organizers, and related siblings that differ by drawers or pen-holder configurations.

## Product-fact gate

Verify each child separately before drafting:

- Tier count.
- Exact dimensions and orientation (`L x W x H`).
- Drawer presence or absence.
- Pen-holder count and placement.
- Metal or metal-mesh construction.
- Color.
- Whether trays are fixed, stackable, removable, or adjustable; never infer these from a sibling.
- Parent ASIN and sibling mapping when several children share ratings or comparison-table content.

A family comparison table may show several products with the same tier count but different accessories. Map each child by its own title, selected ASIN, dimensions, bullets, and twister values rather than assuming the first comparison-table column is the selected child.

## Related-SKU differentiation

Do not reuse one title and merely replace `Drawer` with `3 Pen Holders`. Lead with the verified purchase-decision difference while retaining the shared keyword family.

Useful title roots:

- `7-Tier Desk Organizer`
- `Metal Letter Tray`
- `Paper File Organizer`
- `Desktop File Organizer`
- `Drawer`
- `Pen Holder` / `3 Pen Holders`

Example field roles:

- Drawer child: title leads with `7-Tier Desk Organizer with Drawer and Pen Holder`; Item Highlights explains the exact footprint and storage of paper, notes, and small office supplies.
- Multi-holder child: title leads with `7-Tier Desk Organizer with 3 Pen Holders`; Item Highlights explains the exact footprint and storage of pens, markers, scissors, and files.

The difference must be factual and conversion-relevant. Do not force artificial wording differences when the structure is genuinely identical.

## Short-title and Item Highlights allocation

For the user's current short-field workflow:

- Keep Title at `≤75 characters` when latest-format/short-title language is triggered.
- Omit brand only when requested or when the ongoing session has established a no-brand convention for the batch.
- Put the defining accessory in the title when it is the main SKU distinction.
- Put exact dimensions, material, storage objects, and representative scenarios in Item Highlights (`≤125 characters`).
- Item Highlights should read as one coherent phrase; do not turn them into comma-separated Search Terms.
- Start Item Highlights with a capitalized word when the user requests initial capitalization; avoid leading with a bare numeral when a natural phrase such as `Black Metal Mesh File Organizer...` is available.
- Deduplicate at the **feature level**, not just the word level. If the title already identifies `Sliding Paper Trays`, do not restate that mechanism as `pullout tiers` in Item Highlights.
- Apply a **keyword-first hierarchy**: (1) map every must-keep phrase across Title and Highlight; (2) use the Title for the strongest identity phrase, tier count, and defining mechanism; (3) use the Highlight for the highest-value uncovered synonym family; (4) only then add verified attributes, storage objects, or scenarios.
- For a plain tiered-tray child, a strong allocation is Title=`3 Tier Paper Organizer for Desk, Sliding Paper Trays` and Highlight led by `Mesh desk organizer ... letter trays ...`. This covers `paper organizer for desk`, `paper tray`, `desk organizer`, and `letter tray` without synonym stuffing.
- Prefer `with handle` over padded wording such as `with a carry handle` unless portability itself is a verified SKU-level differentiator. Do not lengthen the phrase merely to approach 125 characters.
- Reject feature-narration drafts such as `... reinforced steel frame and nonslip pads ...` when those attributes consume space that should carry an uncovered core keyword. Those details can remain in bullets or images unless keyword coverage is already complete.
- Reject semantic repetition such as Title=`sliding paper trays` plus Highlight=`pullout tiers`; changing the words does not create new indexing or buyer value.
- Preferred plain-tray Highlight shape: `Mesh desk organizer with handle and letter trays for sorting A4 paper, files, mail and folders...`—keyword-led, natural, and complementary to the Title.
- For multifunction siblings, put the structural distinction in the title and verified component counts in the Highlight. Example allocation: title=`3 Tier Desk Organizer with Pen Holders and Paper Trays`; Highlight=`Paper organizer for desk with 3 pen holders, 4 adjustable compartments and 2 trays...`. This prevents a pen-holder child from reading like the plain sliding-tray sibling.

## Claim guardrails

- Avoid `heavy duty`, `holds heavy files`, `no wobbling`, `long-term use`, or load-capacity claims unless independently verified.
- `Pull-Out Drawer` is acceptable only when the own listing or imagery verifies a sliding/removable drawer; otherwise use `Drawer`.
- Do not import a sibling's drawer, pen-holder count, dimensions, assembly mechanism, or accessory list.
- Use `helps organize` rather than promising improved focus, productivity, or instant access.

## Verification

Programmatically verify:

- Title character and UTF-8 byte count.
- Item Highlights character and byte count.
- Exact title-token repetition.
- Child-to-field mapping for every batch output.
- Visible-field overlap: repeat a defining accessory only when needed for clarity; otherwise use Item Highlights for complementary objects, dimensions, and scenarios.
